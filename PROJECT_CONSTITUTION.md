# Keshav Sandhu — Portfolio Engineering & Design Constitution

## 1. Positioning
* **Primary Identity:** Keshav Sandhu
* **Role / Capability:** Product Engineer / Builder
* **Operational Framework:** Personal Engineering Studio
* **Core Statement:** *"From idea to working product."*
* **Target Audience:** Simultaneously appeals to founders, potential freelance clients, engineering managers, recruiters, agency collaborators, and senior engineers.

## 2. Design Direction: "Editorial Engineering Studio"
### Preserve
* Premium editorial typography (*Instrument Serif* + *Plus Jakarta Sans* + *Geist Mono*)
* Near-black / warm-neutral palette (Archival Alabaster `#F9F8F6`, Deep Umber Charcoal `#131211`)
* Restrained Signal Terracotta accent (`#D34E26`)
* Thin structural lines (`1px` hairline rules)
* Monospace metadata with tabular figures
* Strong spacing & asymmetric editorial composition
* Subtle engineering references
* Restrained, purposeful motion

### Avoid
* Generic developer portfolio aesthetics
* Excessive gradients, glassmorphism, neon/cyberpunk styling
* Random 3D objects, particles, floating technology logos
* Excessive terminal/code aesthetics
* AI-generated-looking decoration
* Generic SaaS landing-page card repetition

## 3. Content Principle
Information must communicate in this exact order:
1. What Keshav can build
2. What problems he has solved
3. What he actually shipped
4. What measurable impact resulted
5. How he thinks
6. What technologies enabled it

*Technology is evidence of capability, not the identity itself.*

## 4. Human vs. Engineering Layer
Every major project supports two levels of reading:
* **Human / Product Layer (For founders, recruiters, collaborators):**
  `Problem → Product → Contribution → Outcome`
* **Engineering Layer (For engineering leads, technical peers):**
  `Architecture → Frontend → Backend → Data → AI → Infrastructure → Performance`

## 5. Personality
Clearly belongs to Keshav Sandhu. It is not an anonymous studio, a fake agency, a SaaS company, or a generic developer template. The studio concept communicates breadth of capability while keeping Keshav at the center.

## 6. Implementation Principles
1. Inspect current implementation before changing components.
2. Understand role in existing design system.
3. Preserve good existing decisions; modify rather than unnecessarily rebuild.
4. Do not create duplicate components when an existing component can reasonably be extended.
5. Keep components maintainable, accessible, and reusable.
6. Do not invent content, metrics, clients, testimonials, technologies, employment history, or project outcomes.
7. Build incrementally phase-by-phase.
